QuietSpeech · Dictation overlay

Five concepts for the overlay shown while you hold Fn

Today's overlay is a plain level bar. These are five distinct directions — not recolours of the same thing — for the panel that appears on Fn-down and disappears on release. Every stage below sits over a fake editor window so you can judge legibility over real content.

  • Prove it's hearing youThe live level is the single most important affordance. If it doesn't move, the user assumes it's broken.
  • Over arbitrary contentVibrant material plus a real shadow. Never rely on the background being dark or plain.
  • Never look focusedIt's a non-activating panel. Heavy chrome, title bars or a caret the user thinks they can type into are wrong.
  • Entrance is ~120msIt appears mid-thought and leaves mid-thought. Scale + fade, from the edge it's anchored to.
  • Three statesListening → polishing → done. Plus a failure state for missing permission or no speech detected.
1

Capsule

The least intrusive option. One line, bottom-centre, fades its own edges. Everything is glanceable in a single horizontal read: recording dot, level, words, timer.

Floating card

The full-featured version. Names the target app, so you can see where the text is about to land, and shows the transcript in flowing lines with a caret. A mirrored ribbon reads as "voice" rather than "volume".

Orb

The ambient one. No chrome at all — just a thing that breathes with your voice and a line of text beneath it. Reads as "an assistant is present" rather than "a recorder is running".

Notch strip

Attaches to the top edge. Because it never overlaps the document, it can be wide and always in the same place — you never have to find it. A discrete VU meter instead of a waveform.

5

Monospace

Leans into the developer audience. A prompt, an ASCII meter, the text streaming in behind a block cursor, and a status line for the polish pass. Honest and mechanical — it looks like a tool.

Recommendation

  1. Ship 2 (Floating card) as the default. It's the only one that answers "where is this going?" via the target-app row, which removes the biggest source of anxiety with dictation UIs.
  2. Offer 1 (Capsule) as the compact setting for people who dictate continuously and find a card in the middle of the screen irritating.
  3. Treat 3 (Orb) and 5 (Monospace) as personality variants — cheap to add later once the overlay has a state machine, since all five share the same states and data.
  4. Keep 4 (Notch strip) in your pocket for multi-line dictation; it's the only concept that scales to a paragraph without growing over your work.

All five need the same underlying data: a normalised 0…1 level, a growing partial transcript, an elapsed timer, the target app, and a state. That's already available — SpeechEngine.onAudioLevel drives the bars today, and the streaming partials come from SpeechTranscriber. So the real work here is presentational: pick one, build the state machine (listening → polishing → done → error), and the others become skins.
